# Coordination and participation: research addendum

Research date: 2026-11-12. Continues the [evidence register](psychology-and-game-design.md). These two primary sources inform additional hypotheses; they do not establish a winning game design, player personality model or optimal group composition.

## R09 — Shared understanding and communication repair

**Source:** Clark, H. H., & Brennan, S. E. (1991). *Grounding in communication*. In L. B. Resnick, J. M. Levine, & S. D. Teasley (Eds.), *Perspectives on socially shared cognition*, pp. 127–149. [Author-hosted original chapter](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan%2C%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf).

**Framework:** Communication includes establishing sufficient shared understanding, rather than merely sending an utterance. The chapter examines acknowledgement, repair and how different media change coordination costs.

**Limits:** A theoretical account with examples and prior studies; it does not test our game, prescribe a ping wheel or guarantee that acknowledgements improve enjoyment.

**Inference:** Prototype contextual requests, explicit acknowledgement and visible agreement revisions. Distinguish delivery from acceptance. Observe whether these reduce repeated clarification without turning play into form filling.

**Inspection:** Original chapter available; grounding criterion, contribution/acceptance, medium constraints and cost discussion inspected. No empirical effect size is imported.

## R10 — Group performance and conversational participation

**Source:** Woolley, A. W., Chabris, C. F., Pentland, A., Hashmi, N., & Malone, T. W. (2010). *Evidence for a collective intelligence factor in the performance of human groups*. Science, 330(6004), 686–688. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1193147) · [University-hosted original paper](https://ofew.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/evidence_for_a_collective_intelligence_factor_in_the_performance_of_human_groups_woolley_et_al.pdf).

**Finding:** Two studies with 699 participants in groups of two to five reported a group performance factor. Its associations included the distribution of conversational turns and average social sensitivity.

**Limits:** Correlations do not show that forcing equal airtime causes better performance or enjoyment. Tasks, group sizes and performance outcomes differ from six-person mobile social play. This is not a validated instrument for scoring our players.

**Inference:** Inspect whether a dominant organiser repeatedly removes others' choices. Test transferable responsibilities and supported muted participation. Do not infer intelligence, sensitivity, honesty or an ideal friend-group composition from game telemetry.

**Inspection:** Original article's abstract and main-text study descriptions inspected. No claim of a full replication review or causal intervention result.

## New game-specific hypotheses

These are our proposals rather than additional findings from the papers.

| Hypothesis | Proposed comparison | Possible counterexample | Protocol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit acknowledgement improves practical coordination | Contextual request/acknowledgement versus ordinary pings | Cards add administration; apparent acceptance is misunderstood | PT-07 |
| Voluntary jobs preserve contribution while reducing confusion | Transferable public responsibilities versus self-organisation | The board legitimises one organiser's control | PT-08 |
| Multiple mission approaches preserve agency | One shared mission with alternative approach/recovery | All alternatives are fake or clearly inferior | PT-09 |
| Resource-bounded combat supports rescue choices | Fixed weapon charges with tracked transfers/recovery | The finite budget simply encourages early attacks or total hoarding | PT-10 |

## Practical observation rules

Ask people to explain the intended plan, who accepted it and what a fact proves. Record failed coordination and useful disagreement as well as completion. Do not assume fewer spoken words, more messages, faster completion or equal airtime means a better experience. Keep observations voluntary and avoid raw voice recording as the default measurement.
