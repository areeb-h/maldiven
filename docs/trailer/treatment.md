# Drift Station launch trailer: director's treatment

Working title: **"What Happened"**. Length 1:47.5 (1:45 in the must-have cut), 30 fps, 2.39:1 inside 16:9, 1080p from a 1440p master.

Companions: [script.md](script.md) is the shot-by-shot shooting script; [production-plan.md](production-plan.md) is what has to be built to shoot it.

## Logline

Six friends bring a dead station back to life, the lights go out, and by the time they come back on the crew has talked itself into throwing the wrong person out of the airlock. The one who did it was standing next to you the whole time.

## The idea

The design doc says it outright: "The timeline is the story." Every round of Drift Station ends on a result screen that lists what happened, minute by minute, and names the infiltrator. Friends argue over that list before they hit rematch.

So the trailer **is** that list. It is one round, told in order, and its only words are the game's own timeline rows, appearing as lower thirds while the thing they describe happens on screen:

> `2:02  Someone triggered a blackout.`
> `3:40  Jon shot Zara.`
> `4:05  Zara was spaced.`

The rows are flat and factual. The pictures are not. The gap between "Zara was spaced" and what that looked like is the game's humour and its horror in one frame, and it needs no voice-over, which we don't have.

The last text in the trailer is the result screen's own line, THE INFILTRATOR WAS, and the last button is PLAY AGAIN.

## What is wrong with the current cut

It was built as a tour of features, and it is a good tour. These things land and I am keeping them:

- The cold open's flashlight sweep in the Cargo blackout, and the red visor waking behind the crew.
- The 120 ms of silence before the power hit, and the lights coming up on the score's bloom.
- The core as a real rigid body in the shove, and the weightless Jolt ragdoll going out of the airlock.
- The countdown cross-cut that accelerates into a hard stop.

What is flat, beat by beat:

| Beat | Problem |
| --- | --- |
| Whole cut | **Nobody to follow.** Eight suit colours rotate through. The villain is a red visor at 0:04, purple at 0:32, green at 0:40 and purple again at 0:52. Nothing set up is paid off, so nothing is a betrayal: it is strangers shoving strangers. |
| Title sting, 0:07 | The title arrives before we care, then again at the end. One of them is wasted. |
| Assemble, 0:09 | 7.5 s of walking to a spot. The wave and the helmet tap are there but mean nothing later. |
| Lights on, 0:18 | 5 s on one slow push. The best music moment is spent on a wide shot of backs. |
| Vault, 0:23 | The core glows and nobody wants it. No hand, no hesitation. |
| "One of you is lying", 0:28 | The line sits on a dimmed picture, then five faces that are all "suspicious", a face that reads as neutral past 4 m. Five identical looks are not suspicion; one look held too long is. |
| Gun, 0:33 | Orange takes the gun and fires it at nobody. The only gun aboard, three shots ever, and the shot costs nothing. |
| Betrayal and chase, 0:38 | The strongest material, with real physics. But we met neither of them before, and the chase ends on a cut to something else. |
| Airlock, 0:48 | We do not know the body. The console shot is a still. Then the drama is over in four seconds and the music never stops. In a game whose own design says "vacuum is silent". |
| Countdown, 0:59 | A 30%-of-frame digit covers the action it counts. |
| Launch and end card, 1:07 | **The crew wins.** The question the game asks (who?) is answered by nobody, and the tension resolves into a shuttle flying away. A social-deduction trailer should end with the audience suspicious, not relieved. |
| Camera | Every shot is an eased straight-line push at FOV 40 to 50, chest height. One sentence, repeated sixteen times. |
| Rhythm | 16 beats in 80 s, an average of 5 s. It is never fast enough to be a scramble and never still enough to be dread. |

## Who we follow

The trailer has three people and three witnesses. Names come from the game's own UI captures; "Sam" is the traitor in the design doc's pitch ("Sam shoved me into the dark"), so Sam it is.

- **Jon** (blue, 2). Us. Open, waves first, carries the first cell. He ends up holding the gun because somebody has to, and he is the one who pulls the lever.
- **Sam** (orange, 6). Jon's friend. Beside him in every frame of Act I, slots the third cell, gives the thumbs-up. **The infiltrator.** He never does anything visibly wrong in the light. He helps.
- **Zara** (purple, 5). Stands a little apart. Watches. Is nearest the vault when the lights die. Everything about how she is framed says guilty, and she is crew.
- **Areeb, Maya, Kit** (yellow, green, teal). The room. They step back when the gun comes up.

### The emotional arc

1. **Belonging.** Red emergency light, a dead station, and six small figures who are glad to see each other. The warmth is real. It has to be, or there is nothing to betray.
2. **Triumph.** Three cells, the power wave, the lights. Jon and Sam did it together.
3. **Doubt.** The vault opens, the lights die, and when the beams come back the core is gone. The beam lands on Zara.
4. **Certainty.** The gun. The ring of friends widening around one of them. A shot. The certainty is the frightening part: we in the audience agree with them.
5. **The wrong silence.** The airlock. She wakes up. She runs for the door and is shoved back. Then vacuum, and no sound at all. Spacing the infiltrator ends the round on the spot. The round does not end.
6. **The truth, to nobody.** Zara, a ghost now, drifts back through the hull and finds Sam at the cargo pod. Ghosts see everything and only ghosts can see them. She is screaming at him. The audience is the only one who hears.
7. **Too late.** Jon runs. Sam turns, looks straight at us, and taps his helmet. Black.
8. **Again.** The result. PLAY AGAIN. The same six in the same room, and five of them take a step away from Sam.

### The tell

Sam taps the side of his helmet, twice, three times in the trailer:

- 0:08, greeting Jon in Commons. A friendly tic.
- 0:29, soft and half out of frame at the edge of the crowd at the vault. One second later the lights die.
- 1:32, to the lens. Black.

The first time it is charm. The second time nobody sees it. The third time it is a weapon. Nothing on screen explains it. A second viewing does, and people who watch trailers twice are the people who send them to their friends. The tap is also sound: two dry clicks that the score picks up as its motif (see Sound).

## Tone

**A paranoid chamber thriller, performed by toys, played completely straight.**

The crew are premium vinyl figures with LED faces. The temptation is to make a comedy trailer. Don't. The film plays every moment as if it were life and death, with real dread, real silence, real stakes, and the comedy arrives by itself because a 30 cm toy in an orange suit is doing something unforgivable. The game's physical comedy (the shove, the ragdoll, the body dangling off a shoulder) is shot like violence, not like slapstick. The audience laughs and then feels bad about it. That is the feeling of playing this game with friends.

Reference points, as craft traditions and not as scenes to copy:

- **The locked-room paranoia thriller** (isolated crew, one of them is not what they seem). Why: for patience. Those films are frightening because the camera holds on a group of people looking at each other. We borrow the long hold and the withheld answer. No shot, set or line from any of them is recreated.
- **Tabletop and toy photography.** Why: this is what the game looks like at its best: shallow focus, long lenses, a low camera at the figures' eye height (1.4 to 1.6 m in station scale), practical-looking light. The key art already works this way, and it is why the stills look expensive.
- **Silent-era physical comedy.** Why: our actors have no mouths and no voices. Everything is blocking: who stands next to whom, who steps back, whose head turns. The trailer is written as a silent film with intertitles, and the intertitles are the timeline.
- **The heist-film reveal**, where the last minute shows what was in front of you all along. Why: the helmet tap, and the ghost as witness.

What it is not: a feature list, a hype reel with a dubstep drop, or a "meet the crew" montage.

## Structure

Three acts, 107.5 s. Times are for the full cut; the script has exact timecodes.

### Act I: False calm (0:00 to 0:24.5). Six shots, slow.

The station, dead in front of the gas giant. Commons in emergency red: six arrive, Jon waves, Sam taps his helmet, Zara stops one step outside the ring. A role card flashes for a second: YOU ARE CREW. The carry through Observation under the stars, Zara watching from the deck. Sam's hand presses the third cell home. The power wave. Light on six faces. A thumbs-up.

Purpose: make the audience like these people and trust the pair. Teach the rules without a word: cells go in, power comes on, there are roles.

### Act II: The lights die (0:24.5 to 1:12.5). Fifteen shots, tightening, then one long silence.

The vault opens. Blackout: a full second and a half of black with sound only. Visors and beams. A dark arm reaching for the core. The empty vault; the beam whips to Zara. Card: ONE OF YOU IS LYING. The armory: a glove closes on the only gun. Commons: the ring, the gun, the raised hands, close-ups that get closer and shorter until the shot. The body carried to the airlock, by Sam, helpfully. She wakes. Jon's hand stops short of the lever. Sam nods. The klaxon runs its real three and a half seconds; she gets up and runs for the door, and Sam shoves her back in. The outer door blows.

Then five seconds with no music and no effects. A body turning against the planet. A timeline row: `4:05 Zara was spaced.`

Purpose: make the audience complicit. They should want the lever pulled, and then hear the silence.

### Act III: What happened (1:12.5 to 1:47.5). Twelve shots, fast, then black.

Commons. Four people waiting for a win that does not come, and none of them has noticed who is missing. An alarm from the east: the cargo pod is armed. Zara's ghost comes through the Cargo wall and stops dead: Sam, the core, the pod. Sam's face glitches from a smile to the infiltrator's red V, and the role card returns, in red. Jon sprints 40 m in a straight line through Docking, past the shuttle they should all be on, cut against the pod countdown and a ghost beating her fists through a man who cannot feel them. Jon in the doorway, gun up. Sam looks at the lens. Tap, tap. Black. One muzzle flash: a single frame of Sam already in mid-lunge. Black.

DRIFT STATION. The O in the logo is a visor; its eyes flip to the red V. *2–8 friends. One of you is lying.*

The result card: THE INFILTRATOR WAS, the slot spinning through six faces, landing on Sam. Then the sting: the same Commons, a new round, fully lit; five crew take one step back from Sam, Zara points, Sam waves. PLAY AGAIN.

Purpose: answer the trailer's question and immediately open the next one. We never see who wins the round. The last feeling is "I know exactly which of my friends would do this."

## Visual language

### Lenses

The studio camera takes a vertical FOV. Three lenses only, each with a meaning, so a cut between them is felt:

| Lens | FOV | Used for | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The room** | 50° to 58° | Groups, geography, the ring in Commons | We see who stands next to whom. Used when the group is a group. |
| **The friend** | 34° to 40° | Two-shots, over-shoulders, the carry | A comfortable distance. This is Jon's eye. |
| **The accusation** | 20° to 28° | Faces, hands, the gun, the lever | Compressed, shallow, nowhere to look but the thing. The suspicious face reads as neutral beyond 4 m, so every face that matters is shot inside 2 m on this lens. |

Act I lives on the first two. The long lens arrives with the blackout and from then on the frames get tighter until the vent, which is the widest shot in the film.

### Camera grammar

- **Act I: the camera is on rails.** Slow, level, eased moves. Somebody competent is operating. Nothing is wrong.
- **Act II: the camera starts to breathe.** From the blackout on, a low-frequency handheld drift (under 0.5° and 2 cm) on every shot. Whip pans with the flashlight beams. One dolly zoom, on Zara as the ring opens around her. One slow roll to 6° at the airlock console, the only canted frame in the film, levelling on the vent.
- **The vent: the camera stops.** Locked off. After a minute of movement, stillness is the shock.
- **Act III: the camera runs.** It travels backwards ahead of Jon, shakes with his footfalls, and the cuts halve in length each time.
- **Rack focus is the trailer's way of saying "look again".** Two uses: Jon and Sam to Zara watching (S04, a lie), and Jon's gun to Sam at the pod (S27, the truth). In S07, when Sam taps his helmet at the frame edge, the focus pointedly stays on the core: the camera is as fooled as the crew.
- **Eye level is the figures' eye level**, 1.4 to 1.6 m, or below it. We never look down on them except twice: the wide of the ring, and the last shot of the sting. Looking down is judgment.

### Light

Three states, and the story is told by the changes between them.

- **Emergency red** (`emergency`): warm, low, pulsing. In this film red is safety: Act I is red and everyone is friends. That inversion is deliberate.
- **Restored white and blue** (`restored`): clean, bright, sharp shadows. The light in which people are accused. Everything terrible in Act II happens with the lights on.
- **Blackout** (`blackout`): black suits with glowing trim, visor faces floating, flashlight cones. The stars are suddenly very present overhead.
- **Reactor blue and core cyan** are the colours of the thing everybody wants. Cyan appears only on the core and on whatever the core lights: a glove, a visor, Sam's chest at the pod.
- **Infiltrator red** (the alarm red, `Color(1.0, 0.08, 0.04)`, harder and colder than the emergency amber-red) appears only in Act III: the pod alarm, the V on Sam's visor, the role card, the O in the logo.

Motifs:

- **Silhouette first, face second.** People are introduced as shapes against light (the reactor glow, a doorway, a flashlight beam) and only then seen. Zara is in silhouette or half-hidden in every Act I shot: that is all it takes to make her the suspect.
- **The dark suit.** In the blackout every suit glows in its own colour, so everyone can be identified. The thief's suit is dark, trim and visor both. The absence is the clue.
- **The planet.** The rooms have no ceilings on this set; the sky and the gas giant are over everything. I use that. The planet is in frame for the opening, the vault, the vent and the title. (A test still showed that from Observation only a small dark moon is visible, so that scene plays under stars.) It does not care.
- **One light source per close-up.** The core, the strobe, the muzzle flash, the pod alarm. Faces are lit by what they are looking at.

### The reactor

The core bloomed to flat white in the last cut. In this script the reactor is never the subject of a shot. The power-on is played on Sam's hand at the socket and on six faces: the core is behind the camera or at the frame edge, and the audience sees its light arrive on people. The eased values already in `restore_power_soft()` (reactor 0.75, glow 0.85) with exposure 0.85 stay as the ceiling.

## Sound

There is no voice in the game yet and none in the trailer. Sound does the talking. Every effect and every note is synthesised by `tools/generate_audio.py` and `tools/generate_music.py`, so the plan sticks to what those can make: tones, noise, impacts, filtered swells, reverb. No recorded foley, no choir, no orchestra samples.

### Principles

1. **Diegetic first.** Act I has almost no score. We hear the station: the emergency ambience, footsteps, a cell seating, suit servos. The world has to sound real before music is allowed to lie about it.
2. **The score is born from the station's own sounds.** Three handoffs:
   - *The hum.* `power_up` ends on a rising tone. Tune its final pitch to D, and the score's first sustained note is that same D, entering under it as it fades. The music is literally the station coming on.
   - *The tap.* Sam's two helmet clicks (0.22 s apart) are the score's motif. After the blackout, two low hits with that exact spacing punctuate each turn of the screw: the empty vault, the gunshot, the vent, the pod alarm. The audience learns the rhythm without knowing where it came from, and in S28 they hear its source.
   - *The tick.* The countdown tick is one per second. The Act III pulse is 120 BPM, two beats to a tick, locked. The pod's alarm call becomes the top line.
3. **Three silences**, each a different kind:
   - S08, the blackout: music cut dead, effects only, close and dry. Scuffles in the dark.
   - S21, the vent: **everything** cut, to digital silence, on the frame the outer door opens. One and a half seconds of nothing at all, then a single very quiet element: slow breathing inside a helmet (filtered noise), which is ours. This is "vacuum is silent" from the design doc, and it is the centre of the trailer.
   - S29, before the gunshot: 0.8 s of black and silence, so the shot is the loudest thing in the film.
4. **Hits are earned.** Five big hits only: the power wave, the blackout, the gunshot, the vent (which is a hit of silence), the title.

### Dialogue and text

No voice-over, no subtitles for imagined speech. Text comes in four forms and no others:

1. **Timeline rows** (seven of them), lower left, small, in the game's own result-screen style. They cut in a beat *after* the event they describe: the picture first, then the flat record of it.
2. **Two statement cards**, centred: ONE OF YOU IS LYING. on black after the theft; ONE GUN. THREE SHOTS. under the armory shot.
3. **Game UI as itself**: the role card (twice), the LIGHTS OUT alert, three ammo pips, the countdown ring, the result reveal, the PLAY AGAIN button. Each is the real component.
4. **The title and the end slate.**

Total reading load is about 70 words in 107 seconds.

## Editing rhythm

| Act | Shots | Average | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 6 | 4.1 s | Long and even. Cuts on movement, never on a hit, until the power wave. |
| II | 15 | 3.2 s | Starts at 5 s, shortens to 0.6 s close-ups at the gunshot, then lengthens again through the airlock to the 5 s locked-off vent. An arch: squeeze, bang, hold. |
| III | 12 | 2.9 s overall | The run is nine cuts in 6.5 s. Then black. The sting is slow again. |

When we hold:

- **S04, the carry (5 s).** The audience has to relax.
- **S14, the ring (4 s).** Nobody moves. Holding a wide shot of people not moving is what makes a standoff.
- **S20, the klaxon (3.5 s).** The game's real warning time, in real time, without a cut. She gets up, she runs, she is shoved back, all in one take.
- **S21, the vent (5 s).** Locked off, silent. The longest five seconds in the film.

When we smash-cut:

- **Into the blackout** (S07 to S08): mid-move, mid-note, to black.
- **The gunshot** (S15 to S16): from a 0.4 s ECU of a trigger finger to a wide, on the flash frame.
- **Out of the silence** (S21 to S22): from the planet to five people standing in a room, no transition. The cut says "and then nothing happened".
- **The taps** (S28 to S29): to black on the second click, exactly as in S07. The same cut twice is the rhyme that closes the film.

No dissolves. No fades except the first frame up from black and the last frame down. White is used once, as a 0.3-alpha ramp across the power hit (kept from the current cut).

## What the engine can and cannot do

Everything is rendered in the real station with the real crew, on the Mobile renderer, on a CPU. That is a feature (nothing in the trailer is fake) and a set of hard limits. I have written around each of them.

**It can:**

- Depth of field, far and near, per frame: rack focus is only an animated focus point.
- Any FOV, any camera path, roll: dolly zoom and canted frames are free.
- Glow, soft shadows from spot and omni lights, fog colour and density per shot, exposure per shot.
- Deterministic animation at a fixed step: the walk and run cycles, carry, aim, recoil, shove, hit, stun, downed, carried, ghost.
- Jolt ragdolls and loose rigid bodies; the airlock door, klaxon light and vent burst; the power wave; muzzle flash and tracer; flashlight cones.
- Eight LED faces with blended changes and a glitch.
- Overlay UI at any size.

**It cannot, and what I do instead:**

| Limit | Consequence | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No mouths, no voices, no lip-sync, no recorded audio | Nobody speaks | A silent film with intertitles; the timeline is the text |
| Faces are simple and read poorly past 4 m | No acting in wide shots | Wides carry blocking; faces are shot within 2 m at FOV 28 or less; the character spec asks for faces designed for distance |
| No arm IK to arbitrary targets today | No hand on a lever, no reach | The biggest single production item (C1). Without it, S10 and S19 fall back to the shove pose and a tighter crop |
| No volumetric light | Beams do not hang in the air | The studio's additive beam cones, kept faint, plus fog density up in the blackout |
| No motion blur | Fast action strobes at 30 fps | Keep the sprint mostly head-on (little screen-space motion); whip pans cut on the fastest frame; an optional sub-frame blend for two shots is in the dream cut |
| No screen-space reflections; visors are emissive screens | No reflections in helmets | Analytic glints (C9); real reflections are a dream item |
| The rooms have no ceilings | Low angles show sky in every room; no claustrophobia from above | Use it: the planet and stars are a motif. Claustrophobia comes from long lenses and bodies at the frame edge |
| Bloom clips to white on the reactor core and bright emitters | The reactor shot blew out | The reactor is never the subject; per-shot glow and exposure caps |
| There is no inner airlock door | Nobody can be trapped behind glass | I checked a test still: the airlock is an open doorway off Commons, and in the game you can walk out during the warning. So the scene is truer and nastier: she wakes, runs for the opening, and is shoved back in. A shove is a real verb |
| There is no vote or accusation mechanic | The crew cannot "vote" | They do not. They decide the way players do: someone has the gun. An accuse gesture is offered as a dream item, pending the owner's call |
| Ragdolls may not repeat exactly between runs | A resumed render could jump | Render each ragdoll shot in one pass from its first frame, or record and replay the bone tracks |
| About 3 s of CPU per frame at 1440p | 3,225 frames is about 2.7 hours of rendering, more with heavy shots | Black and card frames are nearly free; previews at 640×360; render per act; see the plan |

One more piece of honesty. The trailer shows a helmet tap before each blackout. In today's game the Blackout sabotage has no animation. Either the game adopts the tap as a real, catchable tell (I think it should, and it is the owner's decision), or the tap stays as Sam's character tic in a trailer that never claims the tap is the trigger. The cut works both ways.
