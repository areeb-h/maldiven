# Drift Station: full experience blueprint

Version 0.3, 2026-11-12. Expanded vision requested by the owner. These are design proposals, not implemented capabilities. The earlier six-player slice remains the first experiment, not a limit on the game's long-term identity.

## The game we are aiming for
A social space adventure where friends can manipulate a believable station together: carry salvage, exchange equipment, reroute power, control doors, board visiting modules, negotiate access, protect a repair team and escape a disaster. Hidden objectives make some sessions tense; cooperation and playful expression make the space enjoyable even without an infiltrator.

The distinctive promise is a station whose shared systems create situations worth talking about. The game should generate a rescue, a disputed decision or a clever plan without requiring a scripted accusation meeting.

## Experience pillars and counterexamples
| Pillar | What the player experiences | Counterexample that fails the pillar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social agency | Several useful ways to affect a shared situation | Wait for the designated expert to finish a personal minigame |
| Physical cooperation | People coordinate around an object, space or decision | Two people hold unrelated buttons for a long timer |
| Readable uncertainty | Facts are understandable; intentions remain uncertain | Random punishment or a perfect traitor-identification terminal |
| Recoverable tension | An error changes the story while leaving meaningful play | Most of the party spectates for ten minutes |
| A place worth inhabiting | Recognisable rooms, expressive people, convincing sound | A maze of interchangeable corridors and HUD prompts |
| Comfortable play | Controls, voice and feedback support conversation | Four-finger input gymnastics or constant unreadable alarms |

## Signature situations to earn through systems
1. A player bargains for access while others debate whether to override the door manually.
2. Two people carry the only charged cell; a third holds the hatch while a fourth restores the alternate route.
3. A radio announcement gathers the crew; a nearby whispered plan creates a smaller alliance.
4. A rescue interrupts a theft because the medic needs the thief's tool.
5. A visiting cargo module provides a shortcut and a new opportunity for suspicious activity.
6. Someone powers observation windows instead of security equipment and must explain the choice.
7. A shield protects a repair or retreat while a rival tries to interrupt it.
8. A post-round timeline shows that a trusted witness honestly misunderstood what they saw.

These are scenario seeds. Do not script success, force a specific betrayer or secretly change the rules to manufacture drama.

## The social space between rounds
A compact hangar serves as the party lobby. Friends check voice, practise tools, inspect a station model, choose a rules preset and customise a small shared crew emblem. An immediate rematch is available; leaving is straightforward. Later, cooperative mini-activities can make waiting pleasant without delaying ready players.

Cosmetic identity can include suit colours/patterns, helmet shapes, emotes, badges and the crew's hangar decorations. Keep competitive silhouettes readable and make no cosmetic an equipment advantage. No expiring streak, paid ammunition, statistical weapon upgrade or obligation to log in. Progression should recognise participation/mastery without revealing or rewarding private vocal traits.

## Mode family
| Mode | Social promise | Horizon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Last Departure | Repair, trust, steal or secure the core and extract | H1: first complete mode |
| Salvage Shift | Cooperate against station hazards; no hidden enemy | H1 rehearsal, H2 replayable mode |
| Open Hangar | Social lobby, tool practice and party preparation | H1 minimal, H2 richer |
| Double Contract | Teams receive partly conflicting cargo objectives and can negotiate | H2 experiment |
| Boarding Incident | A small visiting ship docks; two connected groups meet | H3 experiment |
| Expedition | A crew's cosmetic hub persists across independent missions | H3/H4 exploration |

Modes reuse systems but need their own rule sets, win conditions and playtests. A successful no-infiltrator mode is valuable rather than evidence that deduction failed.

## Horizons and evidence
- **H0 — prove the medium:** reliable movement, audible people, shared objects and recoverable mobile interruptions.
- **H1 — earn the first memorable round:** one station, six players, one infiltrator, one weapon experiment and complete extraction/debrief.
- **H2 — make the station live:** power routing, a modest event director, intercom, additional utility tools, replayable cooperative mode and richer lobby expression.
- **H3 — expand the situation space:** docking modules, selective gravity variation, boarding contracts and tested larger groups. Build these as bounded additions to the simulation, not an unbounded open universe.
- **H4 — connected adventures:** new station themes and longer social expeditions, conditional on players returning for the core experience and measured operational capacity.

The 70–120 developer-day allowance in the foundation roadmap does not estimate all horizons. Each expansion needs a scoped estimate after its predecessor is measured.

## First-session journey
| Moment | Player-facing experience | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open app | Continue/join/create room; readable settings | Fast launch, no unexplained permissions |
| Join friend | Code/link resolves the intended room; version mismatch is explained | No duplicate identities or stale-room join |
| Voice check | Choose mic/PTT and hear an optional local test; muted route remains usable | No unexpected recording/transmission |
| Rehearsal | Walk, exchange a tool and coordinate one action | New player succeeds without a manual |
| Briefing | Shared goal and private role are separate and concise | Player can explain both in their own words |
| Mid-round | Situation changes with visible causes and multiple responses | Conversation/choice survives under pressure |
| Extraction | Clear prerequisites, countdown and interruption feedback | No unexplained win or control loss |
| Debrief | Outcome, short factual event timeline and optional discussion | Misunderstandings can be resolved without shaming |
| Next choice | Rematch, change preset or leave | Replay is a voluntary decision |

## What 'excellent' means
People understand the rules, can contribute with different skills, trust the controls and have a story to tell. A feature belongs when it improves that experience under actual group play. Feature count, playtime alone and visual spectacle are insufficient acceptance evidence.
